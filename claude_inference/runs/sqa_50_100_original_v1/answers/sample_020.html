# Scope and measurement approach

Nonlinear optical (NLO) properties of materials are commonly characterized using the closed- and open-aperture z-scan techniques, which allow independent extraction of third-order nonlinear refractive index (n2) and nonlinear absorption (β), respectively, under CW or femtosecond excitation.The Z-scan technique is widely used to determine third-order nonlinearities because it measures both n2 and β in a single setup; closed aperture assesses refractive nonlinearity while open aperture assesses absorption nonlinearity.[?] In Ni-based systems, reported nonlinear responses include saturable absorption, reverse saturable absorption, self-focusing, two-photon absorption, and optical limiting, typically evaluated with nanosecond or femtosecond pulses around 532–1030 nm.NiO and related Ni systems exhibit third-order nonlinearities such as SA, RSA, self-focusing, TPA, SHG/THG, and optical limiting measured by z-scan and related methods.[?]
