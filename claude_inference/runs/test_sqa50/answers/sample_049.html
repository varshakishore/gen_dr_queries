Practical takeaway
For Ethiopian yam, the food application that most effectively “maximizes” functional benefits is traditional fermented yam porridges/gruels (e.g., “mas”) made from slowly fermented yam pieces or yam flour blended with legumes/cereals and cooked by gentle steaming. This approach elevates diosgenin and mineral bioavailability, lowers oxalate/tannin (with careful attention to condensed tannins), preserves phenolics/anthocyanins, increases protein/ash while reducing fiber, and yields an acceptable sensory product—all of which translate into improved antioxidant capacity, cardiometabolic support, and nutrient utilization compared to raw or simply boiled yam.
