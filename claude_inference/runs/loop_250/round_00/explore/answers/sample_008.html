    Constant comparisons and memo-writing are often relied upon when analysing data in grounded theory research. Charmaz (2006) defined the constant comparative method as: A method of analysis that generates successively more abstract concepts and theories through inductive processes of comparing data with data, data with category, category with category, and category with concept. Comparisons then constitute each stage of analytic development (p. 187). At each stage of analysis, constant comparisons were made as part of the coding process. The grounded theory that emerges from the data is founded on the formation of the integrative theoretical codes (Charmaz, 2006;Jones et al., 2014).
