seedAutism diagnosis in adults
→ harderWhat is the diagnostic stability rate for adults initially diagnosed with autism who are reassessed 5-10 years later?
strategyEmbed a conceptual trap requiring the system to recognize that the question itself rests on a false premise. The question will ask about 'diagnostic stability over time' in adults diagnosed with autism, which implicitly treats autism as a condition that might resolve or change diagnostic status, when by definition autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental condition. The system must recognize this conceptual error and distinguish measurement reliability (test-retest) from true loss of diagnosis (which would indicate misdiagnosis, not autism resolving).
criterionThe answer must explicitly recognize that the question's premise is flawed by stating that autism is a lifelong condition and that 'losing' an adult autism diagnosis represents measurement error or initial misdiagnosis rather than the condition resolving. It must distinguish between (1) test-retest reliability of diagnostic instruments and (2) true loss of diagnostic status, clarifying that the latter in adults indicates assessment problems not developmental change. Fails if it treats 'diagnostic stability' as though adults can naturally develop out of autism, if it reports longitudinal 'stability rates' without clarifying what those rates actually measure (instrument reliability vs. initial accuracy), or if it discusses pediatric diagnostic stability studies without flagging that those findings don't apply to adults already diagnosed.
