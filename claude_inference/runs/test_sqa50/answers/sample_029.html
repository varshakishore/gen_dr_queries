References

  1. [1] Comparing the Effectiveness between Human-generated Videos and AI-Generated Videos on Learning
    Shreyas Verma, J. Leddo
    The present study investigates the relative effectiveness of AI vs. human-generated educational videos on learning. 19 students between the grades of 8 and 12 were given a video to watch on epigenetics, which they had no prior knowledge of. Eight of the students were given a Visla, an AI generative video platform, video on epigenetics while the other eleven were given a humancreated YouTube video on epigenetics. Both videos had the same content. After watching the video students were given a 20-question quiz to complete. Results showed scores on the post-test were virtually identical across the two groups. This suggests that AI-generated videos can be just as effective educationally as human-produced videos. As a result, AI-generated videos may represent a much more cost-effective way of delivering content and without sacrificing educational benefits.
  2. [2] Designing LMS and Instructional Strategies for Integrating Generative-Conversational AI
    Elias Ra, S. Kim, Eui-Yeong Seo, Geunju So
    Recent advancements in generative AI technologies have enabled the automated creation of instructional content, including lecture videos, slides, and study guides. AIdriven content generation tools leverage natural language processing (NLP), optical character recognition (OCR), text-to-speech (TTS), and image synthesis models to generate high-quality educational materials. Xu et al. (2024) conducted a study comparing AI-generated lecture videos with traditionally recorded instructor-led videos. The AI system automatically converted lecture slides into narrated videos using synthetic speech and facial animation technology. The findings indicated that while students demonstrated slightly higher recall scores with AI-generated content, they perceived traditional instructor-led videos as more engaging due to greater social presence. The study suggests that AI-generated instructional videos may be useful for scalable content production but should be combined with human elements to enhance student engagement. Similarly, Netland et al. (2025) compared human-created instructional videos with AIgenerated versions using tools such as ChatGPT 3.5, Midjourney, and DALL-E. Their results indicated no significant difference in overall learning outcomes between the two formats, but students rated human-created videos higher in perceived credibility and instructional clarity. Despite these findings, the study highlighted the efficiency gains of AI-generated videos, which required only 3-4 hours to produce, compared to 12-24 hours for human-produced videos. These findings reinforce the potential for AI to enhance educational content creation while underscoring the need for a balanced integration of AI and human involvement in instructional design.
  3. [3] The Impact of AI-Generated Instructional Videos on Problem-Based Learning in Science Teacher Education
    Nikolaos Pellas
    Artificial Intelligence (AI) has gained significant prominence in science education, yet its practical applications, particularly in teacher training, remain underexplored. Specifically, there is a lack of research on AI’s potential to support personalized professional development through automated analysis of classroom interactions and tailored feedback. As science teacher education requires skill development in complex scientific concepts within problem-based learning (PBL) contexts, there is a growing need for innovative, technology-driven instructional tools. AI-generated instructional videos are increasingly recognized as powerful tools for enhancing educational experiences. This study investigates the impact of AI-generated instructional videos, designed using established instructional design principles, on self-efficacy, task performance, and learning outcomes in science teacher education. Employing a within-subjects design, the current study included pre-test, post-test, and transfer assessments to evaluate learning durability and transferability, consistent with design-based research methodology. Moreover, this study compares the effectiveness of two AI-generated instructional video formats: one with an embedded preview feature allowing learners to preview key concepts before detailed instruction (video-with-preview condition) and another without this feature (video-without-preview condition). It specifically examines the role of preview features in enhancing these outcomes during training on scientific concepts with 55 Greek pre-service science teachers (n = 55; mean age 27.3 years; range 22–35). The results demonstrated that the videos effectively supported self-efficacy, task performance, and knowledge retention. However, no significant differences were observed between videos with and without preview features across all assessed metrics and tests. These findings also indicate that AI-generated instructional videos can effectively enhance knowledge retention, transfer, and self-efficacy, positioning them as promising assets in science teacher education. The limited impact of the preview feature highlights the need for careful design and evaluation of instructional elements, such as interactivity and adaptive learning algorithms, to fully realize their potential.
  4. [4] Transforming Higher Education with AI-Powered Video Lectures
    Dengsheng Zhang
    The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into educational technologies has revolutionized the production and delivery of instructional materials. Among these innovations, AI-assisted video lecture creation stands out as a promising avenue for enhancing accessibility, personalization, and scalability in teaching. Traditional methods of recording video lectures are often time-consuming and resource-intensive, requiring multiple takes, post-production editing, and substantial instructor effort. By contrast, AI-based tools now offer streamlined workflows where scripts can be generated via large language models like Google Gemini, voices synthesized with tools such as Amazon Polly, and visuals seamlessly integrated into slide-based video formats. Recent research provides a broad perspective on the potential and limitations of AI-generated instructional videos. Netland et al. (2025) conducted a comparative study between humanmade and AI-generated teaching videos, concluding that while learning outcomes were comparable, learners perceived human videos as more engaging and personal. Similarly, Xu et al. (2024) found that students performed equally well with AI-generated videos but reported mixed experiences regarding emotional connection and delivery quality. These studies suggest that while AI videos are effective in terms of information transfer, emotional resonance remains a challenge. Other studies such as Leiker et al. (2023), Pellas (2025), Arkün-Kocadere and Özhan (2024), delve deeper into the pedagogical implications. Leiker et al. emphasized the need for interactivity and coherence in AI-generated materials, particularly in synthetic video production. Pellas (2025), working in the context of science teacher education, noted that problem-based learning benefits from AI-generated videos only when they are contextually adapted to the learners' needs. Arkün-Kocadere and Özhan (2024) highlighted a critical drawback: although students achieved similar academic performance, AI-generated instructors led to lower video engagement, especially in longer content formats. Zhang et al. (2025) investigates combinations of AI voice, AI avatars, human voice and human avatars in terms of engagement, cognitive load, and perceived usefulness. They show that while both AI voice and AI avatar can improve user engagement independently, a significant improvement in engagement is only observed when both voice and avatar are AI-generated.
  5. [5] A Bibliometric Analysis of Artificial Intelligence for Multimedia in Education by Dimensions AI
    Potsirin Limpinan, A. Yindeemak, Rungfa Pasmala, Manop Nammanee, Thada Jantakoon
    Artificial Intelligence (AI) has the potential to significantly bridge the gap between formal and informal learning environments by leveraging multimedia technologies to create more engaging, personalized, and effective educational experiences. AI-driven tools and technologies can support a variety of learning experiences that are embodied, project-based, inquiry-driven, collaborative, and open-ended, thus expanding the contexts and timescales of human learning beyond traditional classrooms (Worsley, 2022). The integration of AI in education has been a research subject for over 30 years, combining interdisciplinary fields such as education, psychology, and neuroscience to develop adaptive learning environments and flexible, inclusive tools that cater to formal education and lifelong learning (Hamal et al., 2022). AI-generated synthetic videos, for instance, have shown promise in online educational settings, providing high-quality educational content as compelling as traditionally produced videos, thereby making learning more accessible and engaging (Leiker et al., 2023). Moreover, AI can enhance modern distance education by building virtual teaching environments that overcome the limitations of traditional teaching models, such as resource constraints and lack of personalization, thus improving the quality of education and stimulating students' enthusiasm for learning (Zhang, 2022).
