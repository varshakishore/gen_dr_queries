Bottom line
There is no dearth of evidence that each component works and that multi‑amendment strategies are effective; rather, the combination of lime, sulfur, zeolite, biochar, and fungal mycelium sits at the intersection of multiple interacting physicochemical and biological controls that are difficult to calibrate in “severe” settings. Because hydrophobic oil fractions resist bioavailability, amendments must be sequenced to first unlock pollutants and then deliver microbial access, while fungal systems demand oxygen, moisture, and balanced nutrients that can be competed away by lime/cations and excessive carbonates. The literature provides the pieces; a comprehensive, reproducible study integrating all five together at field scale remains an empirical and regulatory gap that is now technically plausible to design and test.
