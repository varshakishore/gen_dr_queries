# Proses Metabolisme

## Apa itu metabolisme?
Metabolisme adalah kumpulan reaksi kimia di dalam sel atau organisme yang mencakup jaringan reaksi katabolik (pecahkan molekul bawa energi untuk menghasilkan ATP), anabolik (bangun makromolekul menggunakan energi dan reduktif equivalents), dan jalur ambibolik (bagian yang saling terhubung dengan jalan katabolik-anabolik) sehingga sel dapat bertahan hidup dan berfungsi.Metabolisme adalah jumlah semua reaksi kimia di dalam sel atau organisme; anabolisme mengubah precursor menjadi makromolekul dengan menghabiskan energi (ATP) dan equivalen reduktif (NADPH/NADH) sementara katabolisme memecah molekul bawa energi untuk menghasilkan ATP; lintasan metabolisme karbohidrat, lipid, protein dan asam nukleat terdiri dari katabolik, anabolik, dan ambibolik.[?] Unit energi utama dalam sel adalah adenosin trifosfat (ATP), yang dihasilkan melalui glikolisis di sitosol dan oksisintesis (mitokondria) serta digunakan kembali untuk menjalankan proses anabolik dan fungsi fisiologis berbagai jaringan.ATP berperan sebagai "mata uang energi" yang diproduksi oleh glikolisis di sitosol dan oksisintesis di mitokondria serta digunakan kembali untuk proses anabolik dan fungsi sel.[?]

## Metabolisme karbohidrat

## Ringkasan
Secara mendasar, metabolisme mengatur aliran materi dan energi melalui jaringan reaksi katabolik (menghasilkan ATP) dan anabolik (menggunakan ATP) di bawah kontrol hormonal dan lingkungan, dengan mitokondria sebagai pusat produksi ATP melalui siklus asam sitrat dan ETC; karbohidrat, lipid, dan protein saling terhubung dan dapat diarahkan sebagai sumber energi atau bahan bangun sesuai kebutuhan fisiologis.Metabolisme mencakup katabolisme dan anabolisme; ATP diproduksi oleh glikolisis dan oksisintesis; karbohidrat, lipid, dan protein memiliki jalur yang saling terhubung; mitokondria dominan dalam produksi ATP; dan alokasi substrat diatur untuk menjaga homeostasis organ dan energi.[?]

# Overview

When terminal electron acceptors are unavailable, organisms cannot reoxidize NADH via respiration and instead maintain redox balance by fermentative reoxidation, malate/oxaloacetate routing within the TCA cycle, anaplerotic byways such as the glyoxylate shunt, alternative electron sinks, and compartmental coupling of NAD(P)H/NAD+ pools across organelles and the cytosol.<cite id=90cc5fc6-3,90cc5fc6-4,90cc5fc6-5,90cc5fc6-7,90cc5fc6-8,9f41dc39-1,9f41dc39-2,9f41dc39-6,9f41dc39-7,9f41dc39-9,b43388ea-3>

# Fermentative Reoxidation of NADH

Many microbes and tissues generate external or intracellular electron acceptors from fermentation to reoxidize NADH to NAD+, sustaining glycolysis. In yeast, under oxygen limitation or anaerobiosis, NAD+ is regenerated by the ethanol pathway in which exogenous acetaldehyde oxidizes NADH via alcohol dehydrogenase to restore NAD+, while acetaldehyde is reduced to ethanol, thereby maintaining the NADH/NAD+ ratio and glycolytic flux.<cite id=90cc5fc6-0> Escherichia coli shifts to mixed-acid fermentation under anaerobiosis to regenerate NAD+ pools, producing acetate, formate, lactate, and ethanol as byproducts, reflecting a broader strategy to balance redox without oxygen.<cite id=90cc5fc6-3> In lactic acid bacteria, lactate dehydrogenase links glycolytic NADH to lactate formation under anaerobic metabolism, redirecting electrons to sustain NAD+ availability and growth, and extracellular electron transfer (EET) can increase the fraction of NADH reoxidized externally when an acceptor is present, shifting redox balance toward a more respiratory-like state.<cite id=90cc5fc6-8> Thermodynamically, in clostridial fermentations, ferredoxin- and [FeFe] hydrogenases reduce protons to H2, providing routes to reoxidize reduced carriers when external acceptors are absent.<cite id=90cc5fc6-7>

# TCA Cycle–Linked Regeneration and Anaplerosis

Organisms route reducing equivalents through TCA enzymes and adjust anaplerosis to maintain NAD+ pools under electron-acceptor limitation. Malate dehydrogenase and isocitrate dehydrogenase are central to this coupling; malate–oxaloacetate interconversion and TCA node oxidation/reduction help balance NAD(H) pools depending on flux direction and compartment.<cite id=5be1f875-8> Anaplerotic enzymes such as pyruvate carboxylase and malic enzyme can replenish TCA intermediates, and bacteria can switch to glyoxylate shunt activity to bypass two decarboxylation steps, preserving carbon for biosynthesis while sustaining redox balance and NAD(P)H availability.<cite id=9f41dc39-9> In bacteria like E. coli, deleting isocitrate dehydrogenase redirects isocitrate flux via the glyoxylate shunt, coupling malate dehydrogenase–driven NADH formation with downstream oxidative phosphorylation when oxygen is available, illustrating how anaplerosis and TCA routing interface with NAD+ regeneration.<cite id=5be1f875-5>

# Alternative Electron Sinks and Redox Mediators

Beyond canonical fermentation, alternative dehydrogenases act as intracellular electron sinks to reoxidize NADH. Glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase oxidizes NADH to NAD+ by reducing glycerol-3-phosphate, providing an alternative route to sustain NAD+ pools under metabolic constraints.<cite id=90cc5fc6-5> In lactic acid bacteria, quinones such as demethylmenaquinone (DHNA) can shuttle electrons from the NADH pool to external acceptors, facilitating NAD+ regeneration when respiration is limited, and exogenous DHNA can restore growth in NAD+ regeneration-blocked mutants under anaerobiosis.<cite id=90cc5fc6-4> When an external acceptor is present (e.g., Fe3+ or an anode), EET increases the fraction of NADH reoxidized outside the cell or organelle, raising the NAD+/NADH ratio toward respiratory-like values; in contrast, under strict anaerobiosis more NADH is cycled through fermentation.<cite id=90cc5fc6-6,90cc5fc6-8>

# NADPH/NAD+ Coupling via the Malate–Oxaloacetate Shuttle and PPP

In chloroplasts and mitochondria, intercompartmental malate–oxaloacetate shuttles reallocate reducing equivalents to balance NAD(P)H pools and thereby support NAD+ availability. When NADPH production exceeds consumption in the chloroplast, NADP-MDH reduces OAA to malate, regenerating NADP+, and the malate is exported for re-oxidation in other compartments, which can feed back to sustain NAD+ in mitochondria or the cytosol.<cite id=9f41dc39-6,9f41dc39-7> Under conditions of limited terminal electron acceptors or oxidative stress, chloroplasts activate this shuttle to maintain redox homeostasis, linking NADPH regeneration to cytosolic and mitochondrial NAD+ pools.<cite id=9f41dc39-1,9f41dc39-2> The oxidative pentose phosphate pathway can also compensate for NADPH imbalances, and its modulation can redirect TCA activity to preserve redox balance, as shown by flux-predictor analyses where oxidative PPP activity helps maintain NADPH equilibrium.<cite id=9f41dc39-0>

# Mammalian Ketogenesis as an NADH-Dependent NAD+ Regeneration Route

In mammals, during fasting, hepatic ketogenesis commits acetyl-CoA flux to NADH-dependent steps that regenerate NAD+, enabling continued β-oxidation and ketone production. HMG-CoA lyase cleaves HMG-CoA to acetyl-CoA and acetoacetate; mitochondrial BDH1 then reduces acetoacetate to D‑β‑hydroxybutyrate using NADH, regenerating NAD+, while the cytosolic isoform can reverse this under different [NAD+] conditions to tune the ratio of β‑hydroxybutyrate to acetoacetate.<cite id=b43388ea-2,b43388ea-4> This NADH-linked regeneration of NAD+ is one of two liver strategies during fasting—alongside UCP2-driven uncoupling—to sustain TCA flux and ketogenesis when NAD+ regeneration would otherwise be limited.<cite id=b43388ea-3>

# Synthesis Across Metabolic Contexts

Across organisms, NAD+ regeneration without terminal external acceptors is achieved by a common set of strategies: fermentative reoxidation of NADH (alcohols, lactate, H2) to maintain glycolytic NAD+, routing through TCA enzymes and anaplerotic pathways (e.g., malate–oxaloacetate interconversion and glyoxylate shunt) to balance redox and preserve intermediates, use of alternative electron sinks, and compartmental coupling of NAD(P)H/NAD+ pools (e.g., malate valve, PPP) that sustain NAD+ availability in constrained conditions.<cite id=90cc5fc6-0,90cc5fc6-3,90cc5fc6-5,90cc5fc6-7,90cc5fc6-8,9f41dc39-1,9f41dc39-2,9f41dc39-6,9f41dc39-7,9f41dc39-9,b43388ea-2,b43388ea-3>
