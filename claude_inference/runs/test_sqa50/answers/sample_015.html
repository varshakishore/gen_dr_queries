What peaks in Fourier space mean
- Periodicities and modes. Peaks in a Fourier (or discrete Fourier) transform identify the frequencies (or reciprocal-space vectors) of periodic components present in your data, with their amplitudes and phases encoded in the complex coefficients. For real-valued data, positive and negative frequency bins carry conjugate information, so a true sinusoid manifests in two symmetric locations unless it is DC or a pure cosine at zero frequency, which appears on the real line; this symmetry can create the appearance of “extra” peaks if you misinterpret one-sided spectra or inspect both real and imaginary parts together without accounting for conjugate pairing. You can only limit, not eliminate, leakage; windowing (e.g., Hamming/Blackman/Kaiser) trades total leakage for narrower side lobes and reduced adjacent-bin contamination.
