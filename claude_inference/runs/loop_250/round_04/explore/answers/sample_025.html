Summary
Climate risk disclosure (CRD) influences financial reporting quality (FRQ) for listed non‑financial firms in Sub‑Saharan Africa (SSA) through governance, audit, and information asymmetry channels. Regionally, mandatory regimes and strong governance in South Africa and Mauritius are associated with higher nonfinancial disclosure and improved reporting credibility, while many SSA markets still exhibit low climate disclosure quality and uneven readiness for new global standards. Firm‑level evidence from an emerging market (Egypt) shows higher TCFD‑style climate disclosure improves financial reporting and performance, and audit‑risk analyses indicate that stronger climate governance and disclosure reduce information asymmetry and can increase audit fees—consistent with higher perceived financial statement risk and more rigorous assurance. Overall, the weight of evidence supports that CRD can enhance FRQ, especially where mandated and supported by governance capacity, but effects vary by country and disclosure quality.

1) Definitions and Scope
For this question, climate risk disclosure refers to the reporting of climate-related risks and opportunities in sustainability or integrated reports (e.g., TCFD, SASB, GRI, ISSB/IFRS S1/S2), while financial reporting quality encompasses the accuracy, reliability, and credibility of financial statements and related disclosures (e.g., lower earnings management, audit quality, analyst inference accuracy, and reduced misstatement risk). The focus is on listed non-financial firms in SSA, where regulatory depth and institutional capacity differ substantially across countries.
