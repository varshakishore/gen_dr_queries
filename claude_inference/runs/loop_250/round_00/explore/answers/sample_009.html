Title: Annotated extended review: Polycrisis in political science and international relations, and its implications for international organizations’ approaches to preventing humanitarian crises

Overview and scope
This review traces the conceptual emergence and contested meanings of “polycrisis” in political science and international relations (IR), maps how the concept is applied across contemporary clusters of crises (climate–migration–security; energy and geoeconomics; Ukraine/Eurasia and democracies), and then examines how international organizations (IOs) are organized to prevent and respond to humanitarian crises. It argues that the distinctive features of polycrisis—interdependence, feedback, amplification, layering, and emergent properties—raise demands on prevention that IOs are only partially equipped to meet, though their architectures (standards, mandates, coordination roles, and financing mechanisms) do partially align with systemic, cross-sectoral prevention. Where evidence is thin or contested, the review flags uncertainties and suggests research and policy bridges, drawing on decision-making under deep uncertainty (DNU) and anticipatory governance.
