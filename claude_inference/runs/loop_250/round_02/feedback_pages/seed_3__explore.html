→ harderWhich exercise—origami box folding or outside-the-box knot tying—produces superior laparoscopic performance gains in surgical trainees when directly compared?
strategyRequire recognition that a specific comparison the question seeks does not exist in the literature. The question will ask for a head-to-head performance comparison between two training exercises that have likely been studied separately but never directly compared in controlled trials, forcing the system to recognize the evidence gap rather than inappropriately synthesizing findings from non-comparative studies.
criterionThe answer must explicitly state that no published study directly compares origami box folding versus outside-the-box knot tying exercises in a head-to-head trial with surgical trainees. Fails if it presents a definitive superiority claim for either exercise, or if it synthesizes effect sizes from separate non-comparative studies and treats that synthesis as evidence of which exercise is better without prominently caveating that such cross-study comparison is methodologically invalid due to differences in populations, measures, and designs.
