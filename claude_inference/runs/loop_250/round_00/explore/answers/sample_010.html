Summary and framing
In semi-arid Northern Great Plains (NGP) cropping systems, agrobiodiversity interventions that stabilize yields in the short term—via pollinator support (floral strips, forage/seed mixes, mass-flowering crops) or annual pest regulation (habitat diversification, intercropping, cover crops that recruit natural enemies)—can conflict with long-term soil carbon (C) accumulation when they increase aboveground or rhizosphere C losses, favor C4 pathways with higher respiratory losses, or export biomass outside the field system. The direction and magnitude of soil C change depend on the balance of C inputs (residue and root inputs, rhizodeposits) and C losses (soil respiration, denigration/oxidation, export via grazing/harvest, and off-site transport). Evidence from NGP rotations, prairie/flower strips, and cover crop trials shows that some pollinator- and pest-support strategies boost biodiversity and ecosystem services with modest or early soil C gains, while others can reduce SOC or impose management costs that undermine long-term C storage.
