strategyRequire recognition that the question's central term—'underreporting'—conflates distinct methodological problems that have different causes and require different solutions. The system must identify that research literature uses 'underreporting of race/ethnicity data' to describe at least three separate issues: (1) complete absence of data collection, (2) data collected but not disaggregated or analyzed by race/ethnicity, and (3) data reported in non-standardized or overly aggregated categories. A retrieval-strong system will find examples of each but fail to recognize they're fundamentally different problems being incorrectly treated as one phenomenon.
criterionThe answer must explicitly distinguish at least two structurally different forms of race/ethnicity data problems (such as non-collection versus non-disaggregation, or collection-with-non-standardized-categories versus complete absence) and must show with specific evidence that different policy types target different forms (e.g., funder mandates address collection, journal guidelines address disaggregation). Fails if it treats 'underreporting' as a monolithic problem throughout, or if it describes multiple problems but does not examine whether policies are matched to the specific root causes of each form.
