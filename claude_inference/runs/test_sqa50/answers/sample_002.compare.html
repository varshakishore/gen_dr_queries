## Short answer

The relationship is best described as **largely parallel emergence with partial, one-directional borrowing**—not a clean case of either "IFT caused exploratory search" or "two fully independent traditions." Information Foraging Theory's (IFT) formal models came first and contributed specific transferable concepts (most notably *information scent* and the economic "cost of attention" framing) that exploratory-search researchers later adopted. But the *conceptual core* of exploratory search descends from a separate, partly older lineage in information-seeking research (berrypicking, the Information Search Process, browsing studies), not from IFT's foraging mathematics. IFT functioned more as one theoretical resource invoked *after* the fact than as the generative cause of the exploratory-search agenda.

Here is the evidence and reasoning.

## 1. The chronology rules out simultaneous independent emergence (for the formal models specifically)

IFT's formal apparatus clearly predates exploratory search as a named research program:

- Peter Pirolli and Stuart Card began applying **optimal foraging theory** from behavioral ecology to information seeking at Xerox PARC in the **early-to-mid 1990s**, with their first article in 1995 ("Information foraging in information access environments") and their canonical formal statement in the 1999 *Psychological Review* paper, later consolidated in the 2007 book *Information Foraging Theory* (Pirolli & Card 1995, 1999; book 2007). Their models import **Charnov's marginal value theorem**, patch/diet models, and the goal of maximizing "valuable information per unit cost" directly from Stephens & Krebs's foraging theory.
- "Exploratory search" only crystallized as a self-conscious interdisciplinary agenda around **2005–2007**: Marchionini's 2006 *Communications of the ACM* article "Exploratory search: from finding to understanding," the associated CACM special issue, the CHI 2007 "Exploratory search and HCI" workshop, and White & Roth's 2009 synthesis *Exploratory Search: Beyond the Query-Response Paradigm*.

So the formal foraging models existed roughly a decade before the exploratory-search label. That timing makes a "they arose at the same moment independently" story implausible for the models themselves—but it does *not* by itself establish causal shaping, because exploratory search drew on ideas older than IFT too.

## 2. Exploratory search's conceptual core comes from a different lineage

The intellectual ancestry that most directly defines exploratory search is the dynamic/iterative information-seeking tradition, which is largely independent of foraging mathematics:

- **Bates's berrypicking model (1989)**, which reconceived searching as an evolving sequence of selections in which the information need itself shifts as the searcher reads and learns. Notably, Bates herself drew on *optimal-foraging/wayfinding* analogies, so there's shared ancestry rather than derivation from Pirolli & Card.
- **Kuhlthau's Information Search Process** and **Ellis's behavioral model**, which tie the search process to evolving understanding and problem definition.
- Marchionini's own earlier work on information seeking in electronic environments and his lookup/learn/investigate taxonomy.

Reviews of the field consistently locate exploratory search's definition in *activity and context*—open-ended, multifaceted, uncertain-outcome tasks involving learning and investigation (Marchionini 2006; White & Roth 2009; Wildemuth & Freund 2012)—rather than in foraging's rate-maximization formalism. This is why historiographic treatments (e.g., Jiang's "Exploratory Search: A Critical Analysis of the Theoretical Foundations") list information foraging as *one of several* contributing frameworks (alongside interactive IR, cognitive IR, berrypicking, and "evolving search"), not as its origin point.

## 3. Where IFT genuinely *did* shape exploratory search: concept transfer, not parentage

The causal influence that does exist is real but **asymmetric and selective**—exploratory-search researchers imported particular IFT constructs to explain and design for behaviors they had already identified on other grounds:

- **Information scent** is the clearest transfer. The concept (proximal cues that let users estimate the value/cost of pursuing a link or path) became a working principle in exploratory-search interface design and evaluation, and in adjacent applications such as content-based image retrieval and recommender systems, where authors explicitly invoke IFT to model exploratory strategies (e.g., Liu et al.'s study identifying "risky" vs. "cautious" exploratory strategies via IFT).
- **The patch/diet and cost-benefit framing** gave exploratory-search and HCI researchers a vocabulary for the economics of navigation—why users abandon a "patch," why reducing between-patch travel cost (faster search, better links) changes dwell behavior. This is the foraging idea exploratory-search system designers operationalized.

So IFT supplied *mechanism-level* tools that were retrofitted onto exploratory search's *phenomenon-level* agenda.

## 4. The most authoritative direct comparison treats them as two distinct frameworks, not parent and child

Savolainen's 2018 *Journal of Information Science* paper, "Berrypicking and information foraging: Comparison of two theoretical frameworks for studying exploratory search," is the single most relevant source for your question. Based on a conceptual analysis of about 30 studies, it frames **berrypicking and IFT as two *separate* lenses on the same phenomenon**, with complementary emphases:

- The **berrypicking** model treats *changing information needs* as the prime mover of searching and pays more attention to **focused searching** as a constituent of exploratory search.
- **IFT** treats the *need to perform a task* as the main trigger and concentrates on characterizing **exploratory browsing** via cost-benefit dynamics.

The fact that the field's comparative literature positions these as *parallel frameworks both applied to* exploratory search—rather than as IFT *generating* exploratory search—is strong evidence that exploratory search emerged as its own construct and then drew selectively on multiple theories, IFT among them.

## 5. A shared common cause they both react against

Part of the "overlapping phenomena" feeling comes from a **shared adversary** rather than a shared origin: both IFT and exploratory search define themselves against the static, single-shot **query-response / Boolean-retrieval paradigm**. IFT models adaptive, ongoing pursuit of information through a changing environment; exploratory search (White & Roth's subtitle is literally "Beyond the Query-Response Paradigm") foregrounds iterative, learning-oriented interaction. Convergent reaction to the same limitation produces overlapping descriptions of dynamic, iterative behavior without one having to cause the other.

## Bottom line

- **Did IFT's formal models causally shape exploratory search?** Partially and selectively—yes for specific imported constructs (information scent, patch/cost-benefit framing) used in exploratory-search theory and system design. The influence is genuine but is *concept-borrowing*, not paternity.
- **Did they emerge independently to describe overlapping phenomena?** Substantially yes for the *core conceptualization* of exploratory search, which descends from berrypicking, the Information Search Process, and browsing research—traditions that are independent of (and partly contemporaneous with or older than) IFT, and that share with IFT a common reaction against static retrieval.

The cleanest formulation: exploratory search is **not a descendant of IFT** but a separately motivated research program that **annexed several IFT ideas** as explanatory and design tools. The comparative literature (Savolainen 2018; Jiang's critical analysis) treats them as **distinct, complementary frameworks for the same problem space**, which is the strongest signal that this is overlapping co-development with limited cross-pollination rather than direct causal lineage.
