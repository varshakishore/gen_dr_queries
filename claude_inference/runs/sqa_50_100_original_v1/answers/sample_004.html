Introduction and Background

Student morality has become an increasingly important topic in educational discourse across the globe. In today's complex social environment, schools play a crucial role in shaping the ethical values and moral compass of the younger generation. The concept of "school students' morality" encompasses the principles, values, and ethical standards that guide student behavior within educational institutions and beyond. This multidimensional construct includes not only knowledge about moral principles but also the application of these principles in real-life situations, emotional responses to ethical issues, and the development of moral identity.

The importance of addressing student morality in educational settings stems from growing concerns about ethical challenges in society, including declining respect for authority, increased dishonesty, and reduced social responsibility among youth. Educational institutions are uniquely positioned to cultivate ethical reasoning and moral behavior through their curricula, teaching methods, and social environments. Understanding the factors that influence student morality—including family background, peer relationships, cultural context, and educational approaches—has become essential for educators, policymakers, and parents.

Research on student morality has expanded significantly in recent years, moving beyond narrow definitions of "good behavior" to encompass a more comprehensive understanding of ethical development. This includes examining how students navigate moral dilemmas, develop moral identity, and internalize societal values.
