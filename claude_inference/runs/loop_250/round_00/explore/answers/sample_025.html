    To maintain iron homoeostasis, the iron regulatory hormone hepcidin is tightly controlled by BMP‐Smad signalling pathway, but the physiological role of Smad7 in hepcidin regulation remains elusive. We generated and characterized hepatocyte‐specific Smad7 knockout mice (Smad7Alb/Alb), which showed decreased serum iron, tissue iron, haemoglobin concentration, up‐regulated hepcidin and increased phosphor‐Smad1/5/8 levels in both isolated primary hepatocytes and liver tissues. Increased levels of hepcidin lead to reduced expression of intestinal ferroportin and mild iron deficiency anaemia. Interestingly, we found no difference in hepcidin expression or phosphor‐Smad1/5/8 levels between iron‐challenged Smad7Alb/Alb and Smad7flox/flox, suggesting other factors assume the role of iron‐induced hepcidin regulation in Smad7 deletion. We performed RNA‐seq to identify differentially expressed genes in the liver. Significantly up‐regulated genes were then mapped to pathways, revealing TGF‐β signalling as one of the most relevant pathways, including the up‐regulated genes Smad6, Bambi and Fst (Follistatin). We found that Smad6 and Bambi—but not Follistatin—are controlled by the iron‐BMP–Smad pathway. Overexpressing Smad6, Bambi or Follistatin in cells significantly reduced hepcidin expression. Smad7 functions as a key regulator of iron homoeostasis by negatively controlling hepcidin expression, and Smad6 and Smad7 have non‐redundant roles. Smad6, Bambi and Follistatin serve as additional inhibitors of hepcidin in the liver.
  4. [4] Halofuginone vs the elevated hepcidin hurdle
    Maria M Aleman
    In this issue of Blood Advances, Leal et al 1 identify halofuginone as a suppressor of the iron-regulatory hormone, hepcidin, through a heparan sulfate-dependent mechanism. These findings identify a pharmacologically tractable node upstream of bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) signaling cascade and position halofuginone as a candidate therapeutic for disorders characterized by inappropriately elevated hepcidin including iron-refractory iron deficiency anemia (IRIDA) and anemia of inflammation. Hepcidin, a liver-derived peptide hormone, restricts systemic iron availability by binding ferroportin and triggering its degradation. 2 Upstream positive modulators of hepcidin expression include iron itself and inflammation. With iron excess, liver sinusoidal endothelial cells secrete BMP2 and BMP6, which bind heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs) and the coreceptor hemojuvelin (HJV) on the hepatocyte surface to activate BMP receptor (BMPR)-mediated SMAD1/5/8 phosphorylation, driving hepcidin transcription. During inflammation, interleukin 16 (IL-6) signals through JAK/STAT3 to activate a STAT3-responsive element in the hepcidin promoter, but critically, full IL-6-mediated induction also requires an intact BMP-responsive element, rendering the 2 pathways functionally interdependent. A critical upstream regulator of both pathways is the hepatic HSPG network. HSPGs are cell-surface glycoproteins bearing sulfated heparan sulfate chains that scaffold ligand-receptor complexes across many signaling systems. 3 In prior work, the authors demonstrated that hepatic HSPGs are essential coregulators of BMP/SMAD-driven hepcidin synthesis: genetic inactivation of heparan sulfate biosynthesis or reduction of heparan sulfate sulfation sharply attenuates both BMP6-and IL-6-stimulated hepcidin and causes systemic iron redistribution in mice. 4
