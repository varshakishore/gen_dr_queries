Summary and bottom line
TCEP is a potent, air-stable phosphine-based reductant that can donate hydride equivalents to disulfides and other electrophiles; in iron systems, free Fe3+ can be reduced to Fe2+ by TCEP under anaerobic, acidic, and iron-limiting conditions, but TCEP also readily coordinates Fe and can be oxidized by trace Fe(III) or protein-bound iron, which competes with and often suppresses net Fe3+ reduction in typical biochemical buffers. In practice, whether you observe Fe3+→Fe2+ reduction or net Fe–TCEP complex formation depends on oxygen exposure, pH, iron concentration/oxidation state, and time; ascorbate is often a faster Fe3+ reductant at matched pH, but its utility drops when iron catalyzes ascorbate auto-oxidation. Thermodynamic and kinetic feasibility
Phosphines such as TCEP provide stronger reducing power than thiols, enabling reduction of disulfides and related electrophiles under mild conditions; their hydride donor potential supports one-electron reductions in anaerobic systems. In iron redox chemistry, ascorbate is well-known to reduce Fe3+ rapidly, particularly at low pH and under anaerobic conditions, and can drive formation of Fe2+ that participates in Fenton chemistry; by contrast, TCEP’s reaction with Fe3+ is sensitive to oxygen and iron availability because TCEP is both a reductant and a metal-binding ligand.
