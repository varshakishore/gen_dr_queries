Mechanisms from nanoprecipitation of hydrophilic drugs into PLGA
Nanoprecipitation of hydrophilic small molecules (MTX is highly hydrophilic) into PLGA proceeds via solvent/antisolvent mixing or evaporation, causing rapid polymer precipitation and drug partitioning. When the PLGA fraction is higher (lower MTX:PLGA ratio), the more viscous organic phase and higher polymer availability favor more extensive aggregation and secondary nucleation, producing larger hydrodynamic diameters and higher encapsulation efficiencies; conversely, higher MTX fraction drives incomplete encapsulation, leaves free drug in the aqueous phase, and can enlarge size via unencapsulated drug–drug and drug–polymer associations.
