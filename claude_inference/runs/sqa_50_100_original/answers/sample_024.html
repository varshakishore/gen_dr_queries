Bottom Line

μCP is limited in high-throughput biosensor manufacturing by slow, environment-sensitive stamp/ink preparation and transfer; stamp contamination, wear, and deformation; higher registration/variability than non-contact printing; and the multi-step biofunctionalization and integration layers required for sensitive assays. These constraints lower effective throughput and complicate continuous, large-area processing versus photolithography and robust printing/R2R methods, despite μCP’s strengths in bioactivity preservation, sub-micrometer precision, and flexible, maskless patterning. With process engineering (fast inking, humidity control, mechanical aids), materials improvements (coatings/encapsulation), and hybrid workflows, μCP can be pushed toward high-throughput in niche biosensing applications, but broad, cost-effective manufacturing still favors photolithography and R2R contact printing unless defect/yield barriers are decisively addressed.
