## 2. Core Failure Mode 1: Thought Collapse Under Outcome-Only Rewards

One of the most catastrophic phenomena specific to visual RL is **thought collapse**. When rewards are based solely on action outcomes, RL fails to incentivize CoT reasoning in VLMs, instead leading to a phenomenon termed "thought collapse," characterized by a rapid loss of diversity in the agent's thoughts, state-irrelevant and incomplete reasoning, and subsequent invalid actions, resulting in negative rewards. The agent's thought process often becomes irrational or templated during RL training, significantly impairing its decision-making abilities. Instead of improving, the agent generates fragmented thoughts and degenerates into a strategy of producing similar outputs for different states. Although the agent continues to output thoughts and action decisions, it has clearly lost its ability to engage in meaningful reasoning and decision-making. This is termed "thought collapse." ## 8. Why VQA and Image Captioning Produce Conflicting Results

### 8a. The Reward Verifiability Asymmetry

This is the **core structural reason** results conflict. VQA is a *closed* task — a short, verifiable answer can be rewarded with a binary signal. Image captioning is fundamentally *open-ended*. Applying RLVR to open-ended tasks like image captioning is challenging, primarily due to the difficulty of designing an objective reward function: a good caption can be subjective, with multiple valid descriptions possible for the same image. The design of the reward function is a pivotal factor in the success of RLVR-based approaches. Although designing reward functions for objective tasks is straightforward, developing the reward function for the subjective visual captioning task is challenging. While reward models or the "LLM-as-a-judge" approach have been explored, these models are still vulnerable to exploitation in the captioning task, primarily owing to their intrinsic biases, which may unintentionally encourage the captioning model to produce verbose or brief results. Using reward models or LLM-as-a-judge to provide feedback is vulnerable to reward hacking: the captioning model learns to exploit weaknesses in the reward models (e.g., verbosity or brevity outputs) rather than producing a high-quality response. Moreover, it is difficult to create effective rubrics or evaluation prompts for LVLM-as-a-judge methods because captions are free-form and encode substantial information. ### 8e. CoT Training Data Mismatches Task Structure Differently

Training VLMs on short answers leads to poor generalization on reasoning tasks that require more detailed explanations. Current training recipes often rely on datasets dominated by short annotations with minimal rationales, causing poor generalization to tasks requiring detailed explanations. In captioning, the ground truth is already long-form, so RL's preference for brief, easily-rewarded outputs creates a direct tension with the task objective.

## 9. Long-Chain Reasoning Causes Different Failure Patterns Across Tasks

Long CoT reasoning places a much higher demand on visual grounding than short, single-step QA. To answer correctly, a VLM must repeatedly return to the image, recovering the right object, attribute, relation, or numeric evidence at each step. This makes long CoT inherently fragile: multiple error types can emerge along the chain, including perception, reasoning, knowledge, and hallucination errors. Once any such error appears at an intermediate step, the remaining reasoning can still look coherent while operating on flawed intermediate evidence, eventually producing an incorrect final answer. In VQA, a wrong intermediate step can sometimes be "recovered" by lucky pattern matching to the correct short answer. In captioning, every flawed intermediate claim survives visibly into the final output, making long-chain failure far more damaging.
