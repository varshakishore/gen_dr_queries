    Occupational exposure to vapours, dust, gas and fumes (VGDF) can cause or exacerbate respiratory symptoms and lung disease [1,2]. It has been estimated that ~15% of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) cases are attributable to preventable occupational exposures [3]. Identifying high-risk occupations is important for implementing targeted interventions. Many studies have investigated exposure to VGDF, as summarized in reviews [4][5][6]. Further, a review outlined studies of VGDF identified by a jobexposure matrix [7]. Although occupational titles give a coarse exposure classification, as the harmful agent is not specified and exposure can vary within the same occupation, it provides important knowledge to target risk reduction measures. Reviews have called for long-term follow-up studies of population-based cohorts with spirometry assessments to evaluate lung function and incidence of COPD [4]. Considerable efforts have been made in Norway to prevent occupational lung disease, such as regulations regarding reduction and elimination of exposures, general protective measures, use of personal respiratory protection and medical surveillance.
