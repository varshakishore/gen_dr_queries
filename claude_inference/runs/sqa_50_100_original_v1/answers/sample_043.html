# Short answer
Yes. Preclinical studies indicate that Moringa oleifera contains bioactive chemicals in its leaves and extracts that can promote aspects of nerve regeneration and functional recovery after injury, supported by in vivo mouse sciatic nerve models and in vitro assays of neurite outgrowth in neuronal cell systems.[?]

# Evidence from animal models of peripheral nerve injury
In a mouse model of sciatic nerve lesion, oral administration of Moringa oleifera leaf powder (200 mg/kg) delivered in chow accelerated the recovery of both motor and sensory functions, improving sciatic functional index and muscle grip strength and restoring thermal nociception (hotplate test), suggesting faster functional reinnervation after injury.[?] A complementary study feeding mice with Moringa extracts enriched in polar metabolites (2 g/kg/day in chow from the day of nerve crush) also reported significantly enhanced recovery of motor and sensory function and noted that these extracts helped control oxidative stress and glycemic levels, conditions that facilitate the regeneration phase after peripheral nerve injury.[?]

    Moringa has been widely utilized as folk medicine owing to its rich phytochemistry with special reference to various phytoconstituents such as alkaloids, saponins, tannins, steroids, phenolic acids, glucosinolates, flavonoids, and terpenes. However, the presence of phenolic acids, glucosinolates, and flavonoids are responsible for its anticancer, neuroprotective, and antioxidant potential through various mechanistic routes. It is a native plant of some regions of the Himalayan mountains found in northwest Pakistan and India (Fahey, 2005). The extracts from M. oleifera evince various nutraceutical and pharmacological properties such as anti-oxidant, anti-cancerous, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective and have been traditionally found to be a promising drug for various disorders such as cancer and liver diseases (Abdull Razis, Ibrahim, & Kntayya, 2014;Kooltheat et al., 2014). Interestingly, it mitigates memory impairment, age-related dementia, neurodegeneration, Parkinson's disease, and Alzheimer's disease (Ganguly & Guha, 2008;Sutalangka, Wattanathorn, Muchimapura, & Thukham-mee, 2013). Moreover, M. oleifera exhibits neurotrophic and neuroprotective properties as its leave extract stimulates and promotes neuronal outgrowth and survival both under normal toxic conditions (Hannan et al., 2014). However, the evidence concerning the effects of M. oleifera leaves in crude or extract forms on the nerve regeneration after an injury is scarce until now. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the potential role of crude leaf powder of M. oleifera in promoting the motor and sensory functions retrieval following a mechanically induced injury to the Sciatic nerve in a mouse model.
