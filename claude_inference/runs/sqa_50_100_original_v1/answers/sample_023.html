# Practical protocol: Predicting discontinuous B-cell epitopes with ELLIPro for an in silico multiepitope vaccine

## What ELLIPro predicts and how it works
ELLIPro identifies discontinuous (conformational) B‑cell epitopes on proteins by modeling the protein as an ellipsoid, computing a protrusion index (PI) for each residue, and clustering nearby residues with high PI to form putative epitopes; predicted epitopes receive a PI-based score reflecting their expected surface exposure and discontinuity characteristics.ELLIPro identifies discontinuous B‑cell epitopes by approximating protein shape as an ellipsoid, computing a protrusion index for each residue, and grouping adjacent residues with similar PI values; this framework has been benchmarked against structural epitopes.[1]Rational design of a novel multi-epitope peptide-based vaccine against Onchocerca volvulus using transmembrane proteinsUp to 90% of B-cell epitopes in proteins are estimated conformational (84).     R. A. Shey, S. Ghogomu, D. N. Nebangwa, C. M. Shintouo, Ntang Emmaculate Yaah, Bernis Neneyoh Yengo et al.
    Up to 90% of B-cell epitopes in proteins are estimated conformational (84). After validating the 3D structure of the chimeric antigen, the ElliPro server (http://tools.iedb.org/ellipro/) was used for discontinuous B-cell epitope prediction. The server implements three algorithms that perform the following functions: (i) approximating the protein shape as an ellipsoid; (ii) calculating the protrusion index for each residue; and (iii) grouping adjacent residues based on their protrusion index values. The ElliPro server has been used to test a benchmark dataset of conformational epitopes deduced from 3D structures of antibody-protein complexes. When compared with six other structure-based epitope prediction approaches, ElliPro produced the best performance, giving an AUC value of 0.732, when the most significant prediction was taken into account for each protein (85).
