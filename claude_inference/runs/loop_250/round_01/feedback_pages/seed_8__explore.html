seedAnalyses of how strongly datasets are representative samples of the populations they are sampled from
→ harderHow can we verify that benchmark datasets are representative samples when their target populations are defined post-hoc rather than specified a priori?
strategyExploit an implicit circularity in evaluation: to assess whether a dataset represents its population, you must first define that population, but many datasets define their population POST-HOC by what they sampled rather than PRE-HOC by design criteria, making 'representativeness' unverifiable.
criterionThe answer must explicitly identify the circularity problem: that representativeness verification requires an independent specification of the target population, and when populations are defined post-hoc (by describing what was sampled), standard metrics cannot distinguish a representative sample from a biased one because there is no external ground truth to compare against. Fails if it lists bias-detection methods without acknowledging this prerequisite, or if it treats post-hoc population descriptions as valid baselines for representativeness claims.
